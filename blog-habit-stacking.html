How to make healthy habits
easier to keep

The secret to consistency is rarely more discipline. More often, it’s making the next good choice easier to notice and easier to do.

When a habit feels difficult, we tend to blame ourselves. But habits are shaped by their surroundings: what we see, what we do automatically, and how much effort the next step requires.

Start with something you already do

This is the idea behind habit stacking. Instead of asking yourself to remember a brand-new action, attach it to a routine that already happens.

  • After you make your morning tea, drink a glass of water.
  • After lunch, take a five-minute walk or stretch.
  • After brushing your teeth, prepare one thing for tomorrow.

The existing routine becomes a small reminder. Over time, the new action starts to feel like part of the same sequence.

Make the first step almost too easy

A habit doesn’t need to be impressive to be useful. Two minutes of movement still counts. Adding one fruit or vegetable still counts. The aim is to build trust with yourself, not to win a perfect day.

A good question to ask:
“What would make this choice 10% easier today?”

Let consistency grow from there

Once a habit feels familiar, you can build on it. Keep the action small enough that busy days don’t automatically break the chain, and give yourself credit for returning when they do.

Your plan should fit your life. That’s not lowering the bar—it’s choosing a path you can actually keep walking.
